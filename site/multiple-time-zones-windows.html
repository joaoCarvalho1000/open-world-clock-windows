Show multiple time zones on Windows 11 and 10

Windows can already show a few time zones, if you know where to look. These are the three ways I know, from the extra clocks built into the taskbar to a free world clock that keeps every city in view, with the steps for Windows 11 and Windows 10.

Two extra clocks on the taskbar

Built into Windows. Good for one or two other time zones you check now and then.

Windows 11

  1. Open Date & time. Go to Settings > Time & language > Date & time.
  2. Find Additional clocks. Where it sits on that page has changed between Windows 11 updates. If you can’t find it, press Windows+R, type timedate.cpl, press Enter and open the Additional Clocks tab.
  3. Set up a clock. Turn on the first clock, choose its time zone and give it a name, like the city or a teammate. The second clock works the same way.

Windows 10

  1. Open Date & time. Go to Settings > Time & Language > Date & time.
  2. Select Add clocks for different time zones. It’s under Related settings, and it opens the Additional Clocks tab of the classic Date and Time window.
  3. Set up a clock. Turn on the first clock, choose its time zone and type a name. The second works the same way. Then select OK.

To see them, hover over the clock at the right end of the taskbar, or click it. Windows allows two extra clocks, so with your own that makes three time zones.

The Clock app’s World clock

Also built in. It keeps a list of cities and can compare times.

  1. Open Clock. Select Start and pick Clock from the app list, or type Clock in the search box.
  2. Select World clock. If you don’t see it, open the menu at the top left first.
  3. Add a city. Select Add a new city at the bottom, type the first few letters of a place, pick it from the list and select Add.
  4. Compare. Select Compare and move the slider to see what time it will be in each city.

It’s a regular app window, so once other windows cover it you have to bring it back to check a time. It also has alarms, timers and a stopwatch, which Open World Clock doesn’t.

More than two extra clocks?

Can Windows 11 show more than two extra clocks?

No. Windows 11 and 10 allow two additional clocks, so with your own that makes three time zones, and there’s no setting for a third. For more cities, use the Clock app’s World clock, or a world clock app such as Open World Clock.

Can the extra clocks stay visible on the taskbar?

No. They show only when you hover over or click the clock at the right end of the taskbar. To keep other time zones in view while you work, pin a world clock above your windows.

Is there a time zone widget for the Windows 11 desktop?

Windows doesn’t include a world clock that stays on the desktop. Open World Clock fills that gap: your cities in a small strip, compact bar or vertical column that can stay above every window, free and offline.

How do I see the time in several cities before a meeting?

Type the meeting time in the converter and every city shows its matching time, or open the meeting planner to see the hours when everyone is at work.
